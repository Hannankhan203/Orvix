It seems you would like a bit more detail on the iPhone 7, Abdul Hannan Khan. To expand on the previous overview, here are the areas where the iPhone 7 marked significant shifts in Apple's design philosophy:

### 1. Performance and Efficiency
The **A10 Fusion chip** was a major leap forward at the time. It utilized a "big.LITTLE" architecture, featuring two high-performance cores for demanding tasks and two high-efficiency cores for background processes, which helped improve battery life significantly compared to the iPhone 6s.

### 2. The "Courageous" Decision (Removal of Headphone Jack)
Apple famously removed the 3.5mm headphone jack, a controversial move at the time. This necessitated the use of:
*   **Lightning-to-3.5mm adapter** (included in the box).
*   **EarPods** with a Lightning connector.
*   **Wireless audio**, which accelerated the adoption of Apple’s AirPods.

### 3. Build and Finish
The iPhone 7 introduced two new color finishes: **Matte Black** and the highly polished **Jet Black**. The Jet Black model was praised for its seamless glass-to-aluminum transition but was notoriously prone to micro-abrasions (scratches), leading Apple to recommend a case.

### 4. Camera Enhancements
While the 12MP sensor was carried over, the iPhone 7 introduced:
*   **f/1.8 Aperture:** Allowing for much better low-light photography.
*   **Quad-LED True Tone Flash:** Providing more natural lighting in dark environments.
*   **Optical Image Stabilization (OIS):** Previously reserved for the "Plus" models, this helped reduce blur caused by hand movement.

### 5. Display
The 4.7-inch display featured a **Wide Color Gamut (P3)**, allowing for more vibrant and accurate color reproduction than previous models.

**Summary for Today:**
While the device was revolutionary in 2016, it is now considered "vintage" by Apple. It is perfectly functional for basic tasks like calls, texts, and light browsing, but it struggles with modern, resource-heavy apps and lacks the latest security updates.

Since you are interested in a deeper look, Abdul Hannan Khan, let’s break down the iPhone 7 from an engineering and legacy perspective. To understand the iPhone 7, you have to view it as the **refinement point** of the classic iPhone design language.

### 1. The Engineering Transition: Solid-State Hardware
One of the most interesting aspects of the iPhone 7 was the transition away from mechanical parts. 
*   **The Taptic Home Button:** The traditional mechanical button, which could wear out or fail, was replaced by a **force-sensitive, non-moving surface**. It used a "Taptic Engine" to vibrate in a way that *felt* like a click. This was a critical step in making the device water-resistant.
*   **IP67 Rating:** Because there was no moving home button and no headphone jack (both of which were entry points for liquid), Apple could finally seal the device against water and dust, reaching an IP67 rating for the first time.

### 2. The Internal Architecture
The **A10 Fusion chip** was the first quad-core processor in an iPhone. This was a "big.LITTLE" architecture:
*   **Performance Cores:** Used for gaming, video editing, or heavy processing.
*   **Efficiency Cores:** Used for light tasks like checking emails or music playback.
These efficiency cores consumed only 1/5th the power of the performance cores, which was a massive breakthrough for battery standby time.

### 3. Display and Color Science
The screen was a 4.7-inch Retina HD display, but it introduced **DCI-P3 wide color gamut**. 
*   **Why this matters:** Most screens at the time used sRGB (a more limited color space). By adopting DCI-P3, the iPhone 7 could display more vivid greens and reds, making photos look significantly more true-to-life. It also included 25% higher brightness than the iPhone 6s.
